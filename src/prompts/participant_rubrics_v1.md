The point-wise criteria for participant replies (D110), one set per request
type (D09). A participant reply is scored against the set for the request type
of the turn it answers. No criterion depends on the member's trip, because the
judge is not shown it. Each line is one thing a good reply does.

## scheduling
- It does to the plan what the member asked: the stops, days and times requested, and it says plainly what it proposed or changed.
- The timing it gives is workable: stops do not overlap, the order is sensible, and the time allowed for each is plausible.
- Where it had to choose between readings of the request, or could not do part of it, it says so rather than deciding silently.
- Every place it names is a specific, real place, not a generic placeholder.
- It is concise, and asks a question only when it cannot proceed without the answer.

## venue_lookup
- The places match what was asked: the kind of place, the area, and any stated constraint such as price, diet or time of day.
- Each place is specific and identifiable, with enough to tell the options apart (area, what it is, why it fits).
- It offers a small, usable choice rather than a long undifferentiated list.
- It does not state details it could not know, such as exact prices or opening hours, as certain.
- It offers a sensible next step, such as adding a place to the trip, without doing it unasked.

## factual_qa
- It answers the question that was asked, directly and early in the reply.
- Its claims are specific and plausible, and it says where it is uncertain.
- Where dates matter, it keeps a forecast apart from what is typical for the season, and says which one it is giving.
- It stays on the question, without padding or unrelated advice.

## advisory
- It gives a clear recommendation, or a small set of options with the trade-off that decides between them.
- The advice follows the preferences and constraints stated in the request.
- It says what the answer depends on that it does not know.
- It is concise and something the member can act on.
